# T-261008-09 — Make Safe Guard impact and recovery explanations complete

## Goal
Make Safe Guard trustworthy by consistently answering three questions before or after risky actions: what can change, why it matters, and how the user can recover.

## Do
- Audit warnings and blocked states for missing impact scope or recovery guidance.
- Prefer concrete affected commits/files/branches when already available.
- Show a recovery path when Git Next can safely provide one.
- Keep guidance action-first and beginner-friendly.
- Reuse existing recovery points, undo recommendations, and operation recovery instead of creating duplicate mechanisms.
- Keep Safe Guard concise in the sidebar; details may expand in the dedicated view.

## Keep
- Do not exaggerate uncertain conflict predictions as guaranteed conflicts.
- Do not recommend Force Push as a shortcut.
- Preserve existing safety rules and confirmation boundaries.

## Done
- Major risky actions consistently expose impact, reason, and recovery guidance.
- Recovery advice points to real supported Git Next actions.
- Safe Guard copy remains concise enough for the sidebar.
- Focused tests cover representative safe, warning, blocked, and recoverable states.
